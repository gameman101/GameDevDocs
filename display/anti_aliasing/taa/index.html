    TAA (Temporal Anti-Aliasing)

    TAA is a modern anti-aliasing technique used in games to reduce jagged edges, shimmering, and flickering over time. Unlike FXAA or MSAA, TAA uses information from previous frames and combines it with the current frame to create a smoother and more stable image.

    TAA is widely used in modern game engines because it improves motion stability and overall image quality.

    What Is Aliasing?

    Aliasing happens when diagonal or curved edges appear jagged, or when small details flicker and shimmer during movement.

    Common examples:

    • Stair-stepped edges
    • Fences shimmering in motion
    • Grass flickering
    • Thin lines crawling while moving camera

    Why TAA Was Created

    Older methods solved only parts of aliasing:

    • MSAA helped polygon edges
    • FXAA was fast but blurry
    • Neither fully solved shimmering during motion

    TAA was designed to provide:

    • Stable image during movement
    • Smooth edges
    • Reduced flicker
    • Better modern rendering support
    • Good balance of quality and performance

    How TAA Works

    TAA works across multiple frames instead of only one frame.

    Step 2: Save Previous Frame Data

    The engine stores image information from earlier frames.

    Step 3: Motion Vectors

    The engine calculates where pixels moved between frames using motion vectors.

    This helps align old frame data with the current frame.

    Step 4: Blend Frames Together

    Current frame data is combined with previous frames.

    This creates smoother edges and reduces flickering.

    Why It Is Called Temporal

    The word temporal means related to time.

    !!! info "Across Time" TAA improves image quality by using multiple frames over time instead of only the current frame.

    Performance Cost

    TAA is usually efficient and popular in modern engines.

    Typical impact:

    • Moderate GPU cost
    • Lower than SSAA
    • Often cheaper than high MSAA levels
    • Requires motion vector processing

    Advantages

    Excellent Motion Stability

    Reduces shimmering and crawling edges while moving.

    Smooth Edges

    Produces cleaner lines than many cheaper AA methods.

    Great For Modern Engines

    Works well with deferred rendering pipelines.

    Helps Thin Details

    Improves:

    • Fences
    • Grass
    • Hair
    • Wires
    • Small geometry

    Disadvantages

    Blur / Softness

    TAA can make the image look softer.

    Ghosting

    Fast-moving objects may leave trails or smears.

    Input Clarity Loss

    Some players dislike reduced sharpness.

    Needs Good Implementation

    Poor TAA can look bad.

    Comparison

    Method Quality Performance Blur Motion Stability
    FXAA Medium Excellent Medium Low
    MSAA High Medium None Medium
    TAA High Good Medium Excellent
    SSAA Very High Heavy None High
    SMAA High Good Low Medium

    When To Use TAA

    TAA is a strong choice when:

    • You play modern AAA games
    • You want stable visuals while moving
    • You dislike shimmering
    • You use higher resolutions or detailed scenes

    When To Avoid TAA

    You may prefer another method when:

    • You want maximum sharpness
    • You notice ghosting
    • You play competitive shooters
    • You dislike soft image quality

    Common Games That Use TAA

    • Red Dead Redemption 2
    • Cyberpunk 2077
    • Elden Ring
    • Many Unreal Engine titles

    Final Summary

    TAA is a modern anti-aliasing method that combines current and previous frames to smooth edges and reduce flickering over time. It offers strong image stability and high quality, but can introduce blur and ghosting depending on implementation.
